About Gray

The person behind the practice.

My Story

Like many people, I grew up in a home where affection and love were present, but conversations about sex, desire, and pleasure were not. In the conservative environment I was raised in, sexuality simply wasn’t something that was talked about. Instead, there were quiet messages about being a “good girl,” staying silent, and avoiding conversations about the body or desire.

When I began navigating relationships and intimacy as an adult, I realized how little I understood about my own body, my desires, or how to communicate them. I had never been taught the language of consent, pleasure, or sexual awareness.

Like many people, my early experiences with intimacy were confusing and, at times, traumatic. Those experiences left both emotional and physical imprints that shaped my relationship with sexuality for many years.

When I became a Licensed Marriage and Family Therapist in 2000, I began working with individuals and couples navigating relationship challenges. It quickly became clear that many of the struggles people experience in relationships are deeply connected to sexuality, shame, and disconnection from the body.

That realization led me to pursue certification as a Sex Therapist in 2012.

But over time I recognized something important: talking about sexuality alone often wasn’t enough. Many people needed help reconnecting with their bodies themselves. This realization led me deeper into somatic work.

I became a Somatica™ Trained Practitioner, a Certified Sexological Bodyworker, and I am currently completing training as a Somatic Sex Educator through the Pacific Center for Somatic Sexology.

Through both my personal healing journey and years of professional work with clients, I discovered something powerful: Sexual energy is creative life force. When we reconnect with the wisdom of the body, we access vitality, pleasure, creativity, and deeper connection—not only with partners, but with ourselves.

Today, my work focuses on helping people release shame, reconnect with their bodies, and explore intimacy in a way that feels safe, empowering, and authentic.

My Philosophy

I believe that pleasure and connection are a birthright. Yet many people carry shame, trauma, cultural conditioning, or past experiences that disconnect them from their bodies and their desires.

Healing begins when we create a space where people feel safe enough to explore, listen, and reconnect with themselves.

My approach is:

  • • Trauma-informed
  • • Consent-based
  • • Client-led
  • • Body-aware
  • • Compassionate and non-judgmental

This work is not about performance or expectations. It’s about curiosity, awareness, and rediscovering the wisdom already present in your body.

Beyond the Office

When I'm not in session, you'll find me moving my own body — whether that's through kayaking, hiking, or simply slowing down enough to feel the ground beneath me.

I believe deeply in practicing what I teach: presence, embodiment, and the courage to keep growing. This work isn't just my profession — it's my way of life.
